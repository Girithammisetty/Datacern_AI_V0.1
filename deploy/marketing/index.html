Adopting it

Start on one queue, not a transformation programme

You do not replace a case system to try this. Pick the queue with the worst deadline pressure, run the agents in draft-only mode beside your team, and compare. If the drafts aren't good enough, you've lost a month and no customer was ever affected — because nothing the AI produced could take effect without a person approving it.

What you need to bring

Short list, deliberately. If you can't produce these, the honest answer is that you aren't ready yet — and we'd rather say so before a pilot than during one.

The data the workflow reads

A handful of tables with the fields the decision actually depends on — the identifier, the amounts, the dates and the deadline. Export, feed or file upload; we don't need your whole warehouse.

Your rules and thresholds

The cut-offs your supervisors already apply, even if they currently live in a spreadsheet or someone's head. Writing them down is usually the most valuable week of the pilot regardless of what you buy.

Two named people

One to work the queue, one to approve. Four-eyes needs two humans by definition — the platform will not let a single person do both, and no configuration setting turns that off.

Worth being precise about what runs unattended and what does not. Unattended once configured: the load on its schedule, and the intake rule turning matching rows into cases. Still a person: picking up the case, asking the agent to draft, and approving. We are not selling you a queue that empties itself overnight — we are selling you a queue that fills itself accurately and a team that clears it faster, with a receipt on every call.

And one thing we do on purpose: the install refuses

If the data you supply is missing a field the workflow depends on, the install stops and names every missing column — it does not proceed with a half-configured workflow that silently produces wrong recommendations. You'll see requires_binding and an exact list. Most vendors would call that a bad first impression. We'd rather fail in your test environment than in front of your examiner.
